# What the post announces (facts the post may use)
- A 31-second screen recording: Claude Code below (or left), and on the other side `verdict watch`, a live results
  page showing the script with the changed lines marked, the score history and every criterion.
- A fictional creator's sponsored video script for a fictional budgeting app, reviewed before it goes to the brand.
- The rubric comes from the rules a sponsored video actually has to follow: FTC influencer disclosure guidance and
  Endorsement Guides (disclose upfront, in the video, in plain words; speak only to your own experience; a personal
  result isn't a promise), FTC and ASA rules on free trials (say what it costs afterwards), FDIC and FTC rules on
  credit and banking claims, plus the brand's brief (talking points, code, no competitor names).
- One claim on the must-not list (here: guaranteed credit approval) sinks the score to 0. Past that gate, each of 8
  checks adds about a ninth.
- First review: 0, reject. The agent fixed one failing rule per review: removed the credit-approval promise (17),
  moved the "Paid ad" disclosure to the first line instead of a thank-you at the end (25), "two years" of use became
  the 30 days in the creator's notes (34), "you'll spend at least $300 less" became the creator's own result (43),
  added the price after the free month (52), removed the competitor comparison (61), added the missing talking point
  (74), named real drawbacks instead of "the best app on my phone" (85, approve).
- 9 reviews, 0 → 85, about 2 minutes, unattended. The agent edited only the script and added nothing that isn't in
  the creator's notes.
- Verdict is an evaluation engine: rubrics scored by decision models, humans and deterministic checks; a gate lets one
  failing criterion sink the total.
- Audience: developers on X who use coding agents; also creators and marketers who work with sponsors.
- The post goes out with the 31-second video attached.

## Fictional
- The creator, the budgeting app (Tallyleaf), its rival (BudgetBee) and the script are fictional, made up for the demo.

## What didn't go smoothly
- Approval came at 85, just over the bar. The script's voice is still its weakest score (34%): lines like "the best
  app on my phone" remain, because the agent kept the creator's own words.
