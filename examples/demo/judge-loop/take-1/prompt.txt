Our commit-message reviewer is commit-review.yaml, a Verdict rubric. Before we trust it, check it against people. gold/dev.jsonl has 96 commit messages that human reviewers already labelled good or not good; codebook.md says how they labelled. Work only in this folder.
Call the verdict MCP's check_against_humans with rubricPath "commit-review.yaml" and gold "gold/dev.jsonl". Edit only commit-review.yaml. While fewer than 80% of Verdict's verdicts match the humans, make ONE small change and check again. A change is one of: add or replace one criterion, apply one row of codebook.md to a criterion, or change one setting. Don't apply the whole codebook at once: one change per check, so it's clear what moved the number. Don't copy messages from the gold set into the rubric. rubric-notes.md has the syntax. You may let the judge hand messages it is unsure about to a human, but at most 12 of the 96. Stop at 80% or after 10 checks.
Then check the final rubric once against gold/test.jsonl: 96 messages you haven't seen (don't open that file). Finally print a ROUNDS table (check, agreement, asked a human, what changed) and the test result.
